Conservation and stewardship

Stewardship across landscapes, communities, and public systems.

I am a conservation and stewardship professional focused on helping complex natural resource work move forward across ecosystems, land use types, agencies, and communities.

My background includes Tribal stewardship, GIS, forestry, working lands, conservation finance, field research, public engagement, agricultural and applied economics, and partnership-based project management. Across that work, I am most interested in the larger conservation system: how land, water, people, infrastructure, policy, funding, data, and long-term care all connect.

Whole landscape thinking

Conservation work rarely fits inside one resource area, one agency program, or one technical lane. I am interested in how forests, farms, wetlands, prairies, watersheds, roadsides, public lands, private lands, and Tribal homelands connect.

Engagement and trust

Durable stewardship depends on relationships, clear communication, respectful Tribal engagement, and the ability to translate technical information into decisions that people can use.

Public purpose

I am drawn to civil service and public-interest conservation because strong institutions, careful consultation, and practical implementation are essential to healthy landscapes and resilient communities.
