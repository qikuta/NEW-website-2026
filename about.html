About

A conservation professional who works across the whole stewardship system.

My career has developed across natural resources, conservation, GIS, Tribal stewardship, field research, working lands, public engagement, and public-interest project management.

I do not see these as separate lanes. I see them as connected parts of the same stewardship system. Conservation work touches forests, farms, wetlands, prairies, watersheds, transportation corridors, community spaces, public lands, private lands, and Tribal homelands. It also depends on people working through complex decisions together.

Professional focus

I am interested in roles that help move conservation broadly forward, especially through civil service, Tribal consultation and engagement support, GIS and decision support, interagency coordination, public communication, grants, planning, and implementation.

My forestry, agriculture, carbon, and GIS experience are part of that foundation. They have helped me understand conservation from multiple angles: field conditions, community priorities, technical analysis, public programs, funding mechanisms, and long-term stewardship responsibilities.

Agriculture and working lands

My perspective is shaped by an undergraduate background in agricultural and applied economics, along with a HECUA Sustainable Agriculture in Italy semester abroad. That experience helped me understand stewardship as an economic, cultural, and practical decision-making process shaped by communities, producers, markets, public programs, and place.
